Executable policy models

Hard American problems, modeled so you can check the work.

Each project here starts from a documented problem in the United States, turns a proposed solution into a model that runs, and publishes the results as an open dashboard. Every figure traces back to a source, and every assumption can be changed and rerun.

Method · from problem to dashboard

  1. 01

    Problem

    Stated with measured, cited figures from public sources such as CMS, CBO, Census, and peer-reviewed research.

  2. 02

    Framework

    A written design for the solution, with explicit requirements, performance targets, and named cost parameters.

  3. 03

    Model

    Executable code that calibrates those parameters to real data, simulates outcomes under uncertainty, and tests itself.

  4. 04

    Dashboard

    A public, interactive view of the results, linked back to the model, the framework, and every source.

Healthcare · Fiscal policy

National Health Assurance

A proposed single public system that would replace employment-linked insurance with automatic, continuous coverage and no charge at the point of care, phased in through a gated multi-year rollout. The model computes whether the design's savings can pay for its expanded coverage rather than assuming they do.

Standards

What every model holds to

Sourced inputs

Every parameter carries a citation to public data or published research, and a grade for how confident that source is.

Uncertainty in plain view

Results are reported as ranges from many simulated runs, so you can see how much an answer depends on its assumptions.

Claims are tested, not assumed

A proposal's own headline numbers are inputs to check. Where the model disagrees with the design document, the dashboard says so.

Open and reproducible

Source code, data, and design documents are public. Anyone can rerun the model, change an assumption, and compare.
